The sentiments inspired by beauty are admiration, a gentle charm, desire, love, and a pleasurable impulse.

The soul is beautiful by intelligence, then, the other things, like actions, and studies, are beautiful by the soul which gives them a form.

If, however, we desire to locate the Good and the Beautiful within one single principle, we might regard this one principle first as Good, and only afterwards, as Beauty.

The soul has affinities with the divine and eternal nature.

In this choric ballet, the soul sees the source of life, the source of intelligence, the principle of being, the cause of the good, and the root of love.

Our soul, therefore, is something divine; it has a nature different (from sense-nature), which conforms to that of the universal Soul.

Thought is therefore not the most sacred thing; consequently, not all thought is sacred; the only sacred thought is that of the Good, and this (Good) is superior to thought.

To rise with the body is to pass from one sleep into another kind; from one bed to another; really to awake is to separate oneself completely from the body.

One's real desire is not to possess the desired object without knowing it, but to know it more thoroughly, to present it to the sight of the soul, and to be able to contemplate it therein.

Every life is a thought which, like life itself, may be more or less true.

The Soul is not contained in the world; on the contrary, it is the Soul that contains the world; for the locus of the Soul is not the body, but Intelligence.

When the love of the beautiful overwhelms us, it causes us anxieties, because the sight of the beautiful makes us desire it.

Then, having been well disposed by nature and exercised, we also will contemplate what these souls have been contemplating since the beginning.

Even here below, a life full of wisdom is that which is most venerable and beautiful, though we can hardly catch a glimpse of such a life.

Besides, it is not actions themselves which yield happiness; (the sources of happiness) are states of mind, which are the principles of beautiful actions.

Thus life is a good, not in so far as it is merely life, but in so far as it is real life and proceeds from the Good.

Pleasure accompanies the acquirement of good; but even should pleasure not accompany the good, the good should, none the less be chosen, and sought for its own sake.

The soul must therefore put aside all evil, and even all good; in a word, everything, of whatever nature, to receive the divinity, alone with the alone.

Immobility necessarily begat movement, self-contained life was compelled to produce other life, and calm being naturally exhaled vibrating spirit.

One part is good, the other poor; but the whole is good.

In fact, the soul is the mother of love; now Venus is the Soul, and Love is the Soul's activity in desiring the Good.

By herself, the soul is not evil, and not every soul is evil.

As to virtue, we know it by the very intelligence and by wisdom; for wisdom knows itself.

False opinion rises within the soul because it is no longer within truth, and this occurs because the soul is no longer pure.

Further, when one is convinced of this truth, one should gather oneself together within himself, detaching himself from the body, and freeing himself entirely from the passions.

Nature binds the body to the soul, but it is the soul herself that has bound herself to the body.

Thus, to the good man, virtue shining forth in youth is lovely because consonant to the true virtue which lies deep in the soul.

Such affections, I say, ought to be excited about true beauty, as admiration and sweet astonishment; desire also and love and a pleasant trepidation.

Lastly, bodies themselves participate of beauty from the soul, which, as something divine, and a portion of the beautiful itself, renders whatever it supervenes and subdues, beautiful as far as its natural capacity will admit.

Everyone therefore must become divine, and of godlike beauty, before he can gaze upon a god and the beautiful itself.
